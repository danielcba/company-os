# Cognitive Principles

Version: 1.0
Status: Official
Owner: Company OS

---

## Purpose

Principles are formalized claims that govern the behavior of the cognitive system.

Concepts describe what exists.
Principles explain why the cognitive universe behaves consistently.

Concepts are the vocabulary.
Principles are the grammar.

The Cognitive Lexicon defines concepts.
The Cognitive Principles define the laws that the concepts obey.

---

## The Seven Cognitive Principles

### P1 — The Primacy of Observation

Reality is accessed only through observations.

Observations are immutable.

Meaning is never extracted directly from reality.
Meaning emerges later, through reasoning.

Separating observation from interpretation prevents cognitive bias and preserves explainability.

### P2 — Explanatory Coherence

Context does not create meaning.
It selects the most coherent meaning supported by available evidence.

Company OS never generates Context directly.

It activates multiple compatible mental models.
Those models compete according to explanatory coherence.
The model providing the strongest coherent explanation, relative to the current purpose, becomes the Active Context.

Selection requires confrontation: the active model must always be chosen against the strongest available alternative explanation. Choosing a context without considering an alternative is confirmation, not selection.

### P3 — Stable Concepts, Transformative Intelligence

Concepts are stable.
Intelligence lives in the transformations between them.

Intelligence lives in the transformations of knowledge.

Each concept performs a fixed cognitive role.
The value of the system emerges from how knowledge moves from one concept to the next.

### P4 — Regularity and Law

Patterns reveal regularity.
Laws explain regularity.

The principle defines what is possible.
The context defines the conditions.
The pattern is the solution that emerges under those conditions.

A pattern is a working regularity.
A law is the explanation of that regularity.
Company OS detects patterns and builds laws through hypotheses.

### P5 — Calibrated Confidence

Every judgment that influences action must carry a calibrated estimate of its own reliability.

Confidence is computed, not intuited.

A claim is only as useful as its calibration: what the system believes must be measured against what the system gets right.

### P6 — Deliberate Action

Understanding is translated into action through Recommendation and committed through Decision.

Recommendation and Decision are separate cognitive acts.

A recommendation is advisory and reversible.
A decision is committed and accountable.

Perception and reasoning inform action.
They do not execute it without explicit authorization.

### P7 — Learning Through Outcome

Every decision produces an observable outcome.

Comparing expected outcomes with actual outcomes is the primary mechanism of learning and memory formation.

The system is not defined by what it knows.
It is defined by how it improves.

## Status of the Principles

These principles were first drafted on 2026-07-06 and became Official with the completion of the Cognitive Lexicon.

They remain hypotheses about cognition and are subject to validation and revision.

A principle becomes a law only when it survives confrontation with experience.
