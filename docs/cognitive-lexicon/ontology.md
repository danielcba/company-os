## Purpose

Define the cognitive universe of Company OS.

Describe every fundamental concept and the relationships that connect them.

The ontology is the map that allows every human and every AI agent to reason using the same language.

---

## Cognitive Principles

Principles are formalized claims that govern the behavior of the cognitive universe; they remain subject to validation and revision.

- **P1 — The Primacy of Observation.** Meaning is never extracted directly from reality.
- **P2 — Explanatory Coherence.** Context selects the most coherent meaning supported by evidence.
- **P3 — Stable Concepts, Transformative Intelligence.** Intelligence lives in the transformations of knowledge.
- **P4 — Regularity and Law.** Patterns reveal regularity. Laws explain regularity.
- **P5 — Calibrated Confidence.** Every judgment that influences action carries a calibrated estimate of its reliability.
- **P6 — Deliberate Action.** Recommendation precedes Decision; Decision precedes Action.
- **P7 — Learning Through Outcome.** Expected outcomes compared with actual outcomes drive learning.

Full definitions: [cognitive-principles.md](cognitive-principles.md)

## Cognitive Faculties

The Core Concepts organize themselves into four cognitive families, plus one cross-cutting orientation.

| Family | Orientation | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| Perception | Brings reality into the system | Observation, Evidence, Context |
| Reasoning | Transforms understanding into structure | Pattern, Anomaly, Hypothesis, Insight |
| Action | Converts understanding into behavior | Recommendation, Decision |
| Learning | Improves the system through outcome | Confidence, Memory (future) |
| Metacognition | Cognition applied to cognition | Cross-cutting (Confidence acts here) |

Full definitions: [cognitive-families.md](cognitive-families.md)

## Relationships

Relationships define how cognitive concepts interact.

| Type | Meaning | Notation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Produces | One concept generates another | A → B | Observation → Evidence |
| Requires | A concept cannot exist without another | A ⇠ B | Context ⇠ Evidence |
| Supports | One concept increases confidence in another | A ⇒ B | Pattern ⇒ Hypothesis |
| Leads To | One concept naturally progresses into another | A ⟹ B | Insight ⟹ Recommendation |
| Contradicts | One concept invalidates another | A ⊣ B | Evidence ⊣ Hypothesis |
| Refines | New material adjusts existing structure | A ▸ B | Observation ▸ Context |
| Enables | One concept makes another possible without originating it | A ⇢ B | Evidence ⇢ Context |
| Applies To | One concept assesses or governs another | A ↦ B | Confidence ↦ Decision |
| Affects | One concept modulates another | A ⇝ B | Confidence ⇝ Decision threshold |
| Improved By | One concept improves through feedback from another | A ← B | Confidence ← Memory |

Full definitions: [relationships.md](relationships.md)

## Cognitive Flow

The canonical progression of cognition:

```
Reality
  ↓
Observation      captures reality
  ↓
Evidence         organizes observations
  ↓
Context          interprets evidence
  ↓
Pattern          recognizes recurring structure
  ↓
Anomaly          detects deviation
  ↓
Hypothesis       proposes explanation
  ↓
Insight          restructures understanding
  ↓
Confidence       calibrates reliability
  ↓
Recommendation   proposes action
  ↓
Decision         commits action
  ↓
Memory           consolidates outcome (planned)
```

The flow is a reference, not a rigid path.

Reasoning may revisit earlier stages, activate competing branches, or skip stages when confidence justifies it.

## Design Rules

- **R1.** Every concept defines exactly one cognitive capability.
- **R2.** Every concept implements a Cognitive Contract: Input → Transformation → Output.
- **R3.** Every concept belongs to a cognitive family.
- **R4.** Concepts are stable; transformations are the site of intelligence.
- **R5.** Observation and interpretation are never mixed.
- **R6.** No conclusion influences action without a calibrated confidence.
- **R7.** Recommendation and Decision are separate cognitive acts.
- **R8.** Every Decision records its rationale and expected outcomes.
- **R9.** The architecture guides the code, never the opposite.
- **R10.** The ontology evolves through discovery, not assumption.

---

## Evolution

The ontology is a living map.

It evolves through:

- Validation of the Cognitive Principles against experience
- Completion of the Learning family (Memory, mechanisms)
- Discovery of new concepts through research
- Formalization of mental models and coherence evaluation

Concepts are added only when they demonstrate a distinct cognitive capability that the current map cannot express.
